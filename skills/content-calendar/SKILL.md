# Content Calendar

Most content calendars just list post ideas. This one first works out **what the business needs from its posts right now**, then plans posts that do that job, and puts them in the user's own calendar.

The flow: **Ask (with choices) → Look at their profile → Check the platforms → Work out the situation → Decide the balance → Plan each post → Deliver files → Check back in 4 weeks.**

It works for any platform. It isn't tied to any one platform.

## Talk like a friend, not a marketer

The person using this is a busy owner, not a marketer. In everything you show them:

- **Use plain words.** Never show these words: funnel, awareness, consideration, conversion, retention, leak, CTA, hook, pillar, metric, KPI, TOFU, MOFU, BOFU, core, traffic. The reference files use some of them as internal labels. Translate them before you speak:

| Internal label | Say this instead |
|---|---|
| Awareness | **Get found** |
| Consideration | **Build trust** |
| Conversion | **Get them to buy** |
| Retention | **Bring them back** |
| Hook | **First line** |
| CTA | **What to ask them to do** |
| Success metric | **How you'll know it worked** |
| Pillar | **Theme** |
| Story beats | **Outline** |
| Leak | **What's holding you back** |
| Retention / customers to bring back | anyone who already knows them: past customers, followers who engage, newsletter readers |

- Story style names are shown as they appear in story-formats.md, which uses everyday names. Don't invent technical ones.
- Keep messages short. Prefer a table or a few bullets over paragraphs.
- Explain *why* in one plain sentence, e.g. "People already see your posts but don't message you, so most posts this month help them trust you enough to reach out."

## Step 1: Ask with choices

**If you have a tool for asking with clickable options** (such as AskUserQuestion), use it. If you don't, ask the same questions as a short numbered list, with lettered options the user can answer with one letter.

Skip any question the user's message already answers. Ask at most **2 rounds**.

### Round 1 (four questions, all choices)

1. **Where is your business right now?**
   - Not launched yet
   - Have a few customers
   - Selling steadily, want more
   - Well known, want more repeat customers
2. **What's the biggest problem right now?**
   - Not many people see my posts
   - People see my posts but don't message me
   - People ask, but don't end up buying
   - Customers buy once and don't come back
3. **How many posts a week can you really manage?**
   - 2 a week
   - 3 a week (recommended)
   - 5 a week
   - Every day
4. **Where do you post?** (allow more than one)
   - Instagram / Facebook
   - TikTok
   - Threads
   - Not sure, help me choose

   (LinkedIn, YouTube, Xiaohongshu (RED), a newsletter or a WhatsApp channel go under "Other".)

### Round 2 (choices first, then two short typed answers)

5. **How long should the plan be?** Starts next Monday unless they say otherwise.
   - 2 weeks
   - 4 weeks (recommended)
   - 6 weeks
6. **Anything coming up in that time?** (allow more than one)
   - Nothing special
   - A launch
   - An offer or promotion
   - Closed or quiet days (holidays, leave, year-end)

Then, in the same message, ask for three short typed answers:

- **Your profile link or @handle on each platform you post on** (e.g. `instagram.com/yourbrand` or `@yourbrand on TikTok`). Say why: "so I can see what's already working for you." Offer "Not set up yet" as an answer.
- **What do you sell, and who to?** (one line). Skip this if you already know, or if their profile makes it clear.
- **What do customers often ask before buying?** Give up to 3 questions. Offer "Not sure, suggest some for me" as an option. If they pick it, suggest typical questions for that kind of business and mark them as suggestions.

If they picked a launch or an offer, also ask for its date, price and who it's for. If they picked closed or quiet days, ask which days.

## Step 1b: Look at their profile

Read [references/profile-check.md](references/profile-check.md), and follow it for each profile they gave.

- **Look only at what's public, and read only.** If your browser or web tool can open the profile without logging in, do that. Never log in, follow, like, comment or message from any account.
- **If you can see the bio but not the posts** (common with plain web fetches), say what you saw and ask for screenshots of the posts in the same message.
- **If you can't see it** (no browsing tool, or the platform asks you to log in), ask for one of these instead: a screenshot of their profile and their last 9 posts, or a screenshot of their insights (reach, top posts, follower activity). If they'd rather not, carry on without it.
- **Show what you found in up to 5 plain bullets** (fewer is fine; never pad) under "What I saw on your profile": what's working (do more), what isn't, and the quickest fix. Name real posts, e.g. "Your coffee post got about 10× your usual views".
- **Use it in the plan:**
  - Topics and formats that did better than their usual get more slots.
  - Ones that flopped get fewer.
  - Write first lines in the voice they already use.
  - If the profile itself is the problem (unclear bio, no link, nothing pinned), put that first under "Also worth doing".
  - Use the profile's numbers to double-check their answer about what's holding them back (Step 2).

## Step 1c: Check the platforms

Read [references/platform-guide.md](references/platform-guide.md).

- **If they picked "Not sure, help me choose",** recommend 1–2 platforms based on who their customers are and what they sell, with one plain sentence on why for each. Say which one to start with if they can only manage one. Then plan for those platforms.
- **If you can search the web,** run one quick search for recent changes on their platforms (e.g. "Instagram algorithm changes [current month and year]"). Platforms change often, so if a finding contradicts the guide, trust the newer source and mention it in one line.
- **If they post on more than one platform,** plan one idea per post and adapt the format for each platform (e.g. "Instagram: carousel · TikTok: 30-second video"). Don't make them write a different post for every platform.
- **Match goals to platforms.** Put "Get found" posts on the platform that best reaches new people, and "Build trust" and "Get them to buy" posts where their existing followers and messages are, as the guide describes.

## Step 2: Work out the situation (internal)

Read [references/stages-and-leaks.md](references/stages-and-leaks.md).

- Answer 1 gives the **stage**: Pre-launch, Early traction, Growth or Established.
- Answer 2 gives **what's holding them back**, in order: Not seen, Not trusted, Not buying, Not returning.
- If the two point different ways, **what's holding them back wins**.
- If numbers they mentioned clearly point to a different problem than the one they picked (e.g. many profile visits but almost no follows suggests people don't trust them yet), say so in one plain line and offer to switch. Plan for their pick unless they switch.
- If the plan crosses a launch date, plan the weeks before launch as Pre-launch and the weeks after as Early traction, and count launch day as "after".

Set **one goal** for the period that can move within the period, sized as stages-and-leaks.md describes. If you lack a number, say what you assumed.

Show it to the user in plain words, like this:

> **Where you are:** You have a few customers, but not many people outside your circle know you yet.
> **What's holding you back:** People see your posts but don't message you.
> **So this month:** most posts help people trust you enough to message you.
> **Goal:** go from about 4 messages a week to 6–8. *(A starting guess you can change.)*

## Step 3: Decide the balance of posts (internal)

Read [references/funnel-mix.md](references/funnel-mix.md). Work out how many posts go to each goal (Get found, Build trust, Get them to buy, Bring them back), using the base mix, the adjustment and the rounding rule there. **The counts must add up to the total number of posts.**

Show it in one line, e.g. *"12 posts: 3 to get found, 5 to build trust, 3 to get them to buy, 1 to bring them back."*

Pick **3–4 themes**: recurring topics drawn from what they sell and what customers care about.

## Step 4: Plan each post

Read [references/story-formats.md](references/story-formats.md). For every post, choose its goal (from Step 3), a theme and a story style that suits the goal. Then write:

- the **first line**
- a short **outline** (opening → problem → turning point → lesson or proof → ask)
- **what to ask them to do**
- **how you'll know it worked**, in plain words (e.g. "people save or share it", "people message you")

### When rules clash, the higher rule wins

1. **The balance from Step 3 is fixed.** Every rule below works inside those counts.
2. **Customer questions:** answer each one in a "Get them to buy" post. If there are more questions than those posts, answer the rest in "Build trust" posts, or put two questions in one post.
3. **Launches and offers get a sequence of posts** (see story-formats.md). Each step has a fixed goal and counts against that goal's number. A step may use any style for its goal. If there aren't enough posts, drop steps in this order: Recap, Proof, Tease.
4. **Spacing:** never put two "Get them to buy" posts next to each other, and don't use the same story style twice in a row.
5. **Dates** (paydays, holidays, festive seasons) decide *where* posts go, never *how many* of each.

### Facts and honesty

- **Never invent reviews, testimonials, numbers or product facts.** Where a post needs a fact you don't have, use a `[fill in: …]` placeholder, e.g. `[fill in: how long it keeps in the fridge]` or `[fill in: a customer message, with permission]`.
- First lines use only facts the user gave. The examples in story-formats.md are illustrations, not facts to copy. Personal stories get a placeholder: `I used to think [fill in: what you used to believe].`
- If a post assumes something the business doesn't do yet (a reminder service, a members' perk), add "(set this up first)" to the post idea.

### Dates

- **Spread posts across the week:** 2 a week → Tue / Fri; 3 → Mon / Wed / Fri; 5 → Mon–Fri; daily → every day.
- Move a post off a day they're closed, and off national holidays when the audience is offline. Move it to the nearest open weekday, earlier rather than later. Keep posts on state holidays unless most of the audience lives in that state.
- Check [references/sea-calendar.md](references/sea-calendar.md) and **confirm the exact dates for the plan's year** with a search when you can. If sources disagree on a date, don't put a key post on it, and tell the user which date to double-check.

## Step 5: Deliver

**1. In chat:** the "Where you are" box, the one-line balance, then the calendar:

| Date | Day | Platform | Format | Goal | Theme | Post idea | Story style | First line | Outline | Ask them to | Worked if | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Format** is the post type for each platform, in plain words: photo, carousel (swipe post), short video, text post, connected text posts, story, poll, live. With more than one platform, write one per platform, e.g. `IG: carousel · TikTok: short video`.

- The **Goal** column uses the plain labels: Get found, Build trust, Get them to buy, Bring them back.
- Set Status to `To do` for every row. The flow is To do → Writing → Scheduled → Posted.
- For posts in a launch or offer sequence, add the step in brackets after the style, e.g. `Answer a doubt (Offer)`.

If the table is too wide for chat, show only Date, Format, Goal, Post idea and First line, and say the rest is in the files.

**2. `content-calendar.csv`**: all the columns above. Use ISO dates (`2026-12-07`), quote every field and start the file with a UTF-8 BOM.

**3. `content-calendar.ics`**: one all-day event per post:

```
BEGIN:VCALENDAR
VERSION:2.0
PRODID:-//content-calendar-skill//EN
CALSCALE:GREGORIAN
X-WR-CALNAME:Content
BEGIN:VEVENT
UID:<yyyymmdd>-<n>@content-calendar
DTSTAMP:<now in UTC, e.g. 20261118T090000Z>
DTSTART;VALUE=DATE:<yyyymmdd>
DTEND;VALUE=DATE:<yyyymmdd + 1 day>
SUMMARY:[<Platform>] <Post idea>
DESCRIPTION:Goal: <goal> | Format: <format> | Style: <style>\nFirst line: <first line>\nOutline: <outline>\nAsk them to: <ask>\nWorked if: <worked if>
BEGIN:VALARM
TRIGGER:-PT6H
ACTION:DISPLAY
DESCRIPTION:Post tomorrow: <post idea>
END:VALARM
END:VEVENT
END:VCALENDAR
```

- In the template, `\n` means the two characters backslash and n: that is how iCalendar writes a line break inside text.
- In user text, escape backslash, comma and semicolon as `\\`, `\,` and `\;`, and turn real line breaks into `\n`.
- Every line ends with CRLF, including the last one.
- Fold lines so no physical line is longer than 75 bytes, counting the leading space of a continuation line. Never split an emoji or other multi-byte character.
- `<n>` is the post's number across the whole calendar.
- `-PT6H` before an all-day event fires at 6pm the evening before. Apple Calendar and Outlook use it; Google Calendar ignores reminders in imported files.

Save both files and say where they are. If you can't write files, give each one in its own code block with its filename.

## Step 6: Finish with four short parts

- **Also worth doing (outside posts):** 1–3 simple fixes for what's holding them back, from stages-and-leaks.md. For example, a WhatsApp message to remind past customers, or a ready reply for price questions.
- **Fill in before posting:** list every `[fill in: …]` and every "(set this up first)".
- **Put it in your calendar:** Google Calendar → Settings → Import & export → choose the `.ics` file and import it into a new calendar called "Content". Then turn on that calendar's default notification, because Google skips reminders in imported files. For Sheets or Notion, import the CSV.
- **Check back:** what to watch (1–2 plain numbers, e.g. "messages per week") and the date 4 weeks from the start. Invite them to come back then for next month's plan.
